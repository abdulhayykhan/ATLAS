Which places on Earth are most like the Moon's future base sites?

NASA plans permanent bases at four lunar polar craters — Peary, Shackleton, de Gerlache and Amundsen. Before hardware and crews go there, they must be tested somewhere on Earth that behaves the same way. Following Challenge Guide #09, ATLAS is a discovery engine for NEW analog sites: the world-famous analogs are deliberately excluded, and 14 lesser-known extreme places are measured and ranked against the bases instead.

Sun visibility and Earth visibility are deliberately not scored — Earth has no counterpart for them. They are mission context on the Moon Bases page. A soil bar drawn at half height means the pillar was not scored for that site (no SoilGrids coverage) — missing data is never scored as zero.

And Mars?

The same terrain method runs on Mars data. Jezero Crater (Perseverance's home) was measured from NASA's MOLA instrument exactly the way the Moon bases were measured — fetched as a ~3 MB window of the PDS MEGDR tile by HTTP range requests, not a bulk download.
